Lecture 3

Which momentum? Self-adjointness is a choice

What this lecture does

The rule −iℏψ′-\ii\hbar\psi'−iℏψ′ becomes different operators on different domains. On the circle it is self-adjoint. In the box the Dirichlet momentum is only symmetric, and its self-adjoint extensions are the momenta PθP_\thetaPθ​. We compare their readings and relate finite mean energy to the Dirichlet domain.

3.2 The class A\mathcal AA and the boundary form

Summary

  • The momentum on a circle is self-adjoint (Proposition 3.2), and the ring’s levels tend to the circle’s (§3.1).
  • The momentum with walls PDP_DPD​ is symmetric, its adjoint has every complex number as an eigenvalue, and it is not self-adjoint (Propositions 3.6 and 3.7).
  • Self-adjoint momenta in a box: each PθP_\thetaPθ​ is self-adjoint, with the eigenbasis eθ,ke_{\theta,k}eθ,k​, and every self-adjoint extension of PDP_DPD​ is one of them (Theorem 3.10 and Proposition 3.11). A flux β\betaβ per hop on the ring gives the boundary condition of PθP_\thetaPθ​ with θ≡−Nβ\theta\equiv-N\betaθ≡−Nβ (§3.4).
  • One state, two momentum distributions: u1u_1u1​ reads 000 under P0P_0P0​ with probability 8/π28/\pi^28/π2, and ±πℏ/L\pm\pi\hbar/L±πℏ/L under PπP_\piPπ​ with probability 12\tfrac1221​ each (Example 3.12).
