The box in time

What this lecture does

Every box state evolves, including states outside the Hamiltonian domain. The Schrödinger equation holds exactly on that domain. We compute an oscillating position density and the revivals, then apply the interval reading postulate. For finite sums of levels, the walls alone change the mean momentum.

2.2 Two levels beat

A stationary wave has a density that does not move. The evolved state Utun=e−iEnt/ℏunU_tu_n=e^{-\ii E_nt/\hbar}u_nUt​un​=e−iEn​t/ℏun​ differs from unu_nun​ by a phase, so its density ∣(Utun)(x)∣2=un(x)2\abs{(U_tu_n)(x)}^2=u_n(x)^2∣(Ut​un​)(x)∣2=un​(x)2 is constant in time. A superposition of two levels does move.

Revivals have been seen in atoms. Its levels are not those of a box. The packet spreads out and later reassembles, and Yeazell, Mallalieu and Stroud observed this collapse and revival in 1990. Figure 2.2 shows the first twelfth of one. Berry studied their fractal structure in 1996.

2.4 Reading position on an interval

On the chain of §1.1, the postulates of Quantum Mechanics I answer it. The reading “the particle is on one of the sites in the set SSS” has the projection ∑j∈S∣ej⟩ ⁣⟨ej∣\sum_{j\in S}\proj{e_j}∑j∈S​∣ej​⟩⟨ej​∣ and the probability ∑j∈S∣ψj∣2\sum_{j\in S}\abs{\psi_j}^2∑j∈S​∣ψj​∣2. Foundations describes such readings, and Lecture 5 puts the interval rule into the general rule for every observable.
