Lecture 2

The box in time

Every box state evolves, including states outside the Hamiltonian domain. The Schrödinger equation holds exactly on that domain. We compute an oscillating position density and the revivals, then apply the interval reading postulate. For finite sums of levels, the walls alone change the mean momentum.

2.2 Two levels beat

A stationary wave has a density that does not move. The evolved state Utun=e−iEnt/ℏunU_tu_n=e^{-\ii E_nt/\hbar}u_nUt​un​=e−iEn​t/ℏun​ differs from unu_nun​ by a phase, so its density ∣(Utun)(x)∣2=un(x)2\abs{(U_tu_n)(x)}^2=u_n(x)^2∣(Ut​un​)(x)∣2=un​(x)2 is constant in time. A superposition of two levels does move.

Revivals have been seen in atoms. A short laser pulse puts an electron of a potassium atom into a superposition of highly excited levels, a wave packet that moves in and out, towards the nucleus and away from it. Its levels are not those of a box. The packet spreads out and later reassembles, and Yeazell, Mallalieu and Stroud observed this collapse and revival in 1990. In the box, the density ∣ψt(x)∣2\abs{\psi_t(x)}^2∣ψt​(x)∣2 drawn over xxx and ttt up to the time TTT shows regular patterns of lines, now called quantum carpets. Figure 2.2 shows the first twelfth of one. Berry studied their fractal structure in 1996.
