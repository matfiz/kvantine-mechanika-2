Lecture 5

Readings on intervals: spectral measures

By the spectral theorem, a cited result, self-adjoint operators correspond to spectral measures. We find the spectral measure explicitly for the position and the momentum and for every self-adjoint operator with an eigenbasis. The momentum with walls lies inside the integral of a spectral measure for every boundary condition, so a symmetric operator need not decide its readings.

5.5 Position and momentum cannot both be bounded

The theorem concerns the intervals that hold the readings, while Theorem 4.9 concerns their spreads.

In 2009 Bakr, Gillen, Peng, Fölling and Greiner imaged single atoms of rubidium-87 held in an optical lattice, a pattern of laser light whose potential minima form a square grid. The grid had the period 640 640\,640nm, and their microscope resolved about 600 600\,600nm, so each image showed which sites held an atom. In 2010 Sherson, Weitenberg, Endres, Cheneau, Bloch and Kuhr did the same in a lattice of period 532 532\,532nm. For a single atom, the question “is the atom on this site?” is a reading of its position on a square cell of the lattice, the two-dimensional form of a reading on an interval. Atoms on one site are lost in pairs during the imaging, so each image shows whether the number of atoms on a site was odd or even.

Readings on intervals

Observables: by the cited spectral theorem, every self-adjoint AAA is ∫λ dEA\int\lambda\,\dd\pvm A∫λdEA​ for exactly one spectral measure EA\pvm AEA​, and D(A)\dom AD(A) holds the vectors of finite mean square (Theorem 5.3). Summary

  • Spectral measures: projections on intervals with the defining properties, extended to the Borel sets by a cited theorem (Definition 5.1). An eigenbasis, the indicators on the line and their unitary images give spectral measures (Proposition 5.2).
  • The spectral theorem: the spectral measures match the self-adjoint operators one to one, with D(A)\dom AD(A) the vectors of finite mean square (Theorem 5.3, cited). The spectral measure is explicit for QQQ, PPP and every self-adjoint operator with an eigenbasis (Proposition 5.4).
  • Readings on intervals: probability ∥EA(I)ψ∥2\norm{\pvm A(I)\psi}^2∥EA​(I)ψ∥2, and a yes leaves EA(I)ψ/∥EA(I)ψ∥\pvm A(I)\psi/\norm{\pvm A(I)\psi}EA​(I)ψ/∥EA​(I)ψ∥ (Postulate 5.5). A Gaussian read through a window leaves ΔP=∞\Delta P=\inftyΔP=∞ (Example 5.6).
  • The momentum with walls lies inside the integral of every EθE_\thetaEθ​, so a symmetric operator need not decide its readings (Proposition 5.7).
  • Position and momentum cannot both be bounded: a nonzero state that vanishes outside a bounded interval has a transform that vanishes on no interval of positive length (Theorem 5.8).
