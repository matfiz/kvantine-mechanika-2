Lecture 8

The harmonic oscillator and coherent states

The Hermite functions form a complete eigenbasis for the harmonic oscillator. Its energy is the only self-adjoint operator that acts by the oscillator rule on rapidly decreasing functions. Its evolution is periodic, and a quarter period is a Fourier transform, up to a change of scale and a phase. Coherent states follow classical orbits without spreading.

The harmonic oscillator

States: the Hermite functions are an orthonormal basis of L2(R)\Ltwo(\R)L2(R) (Theorem 8.4). Coherent states are Gaussian packets labelled by the points of phase space, and two of them are never orthogonal. Observables: the oscillator’s energy is the only self-adjoint operator that acts on S(R)\mathcal S(\R)S(R) by −(ℏ2/2m)ψ′′+(mν2/2)x2ψ-(\hbar^2/2m)\psi''+(m\nu^2/2)x^2\psi−(ℏ2/2m)ψ′′+(mν2/2)x2ψ. Its levels ℏν(n+12)\hbar\nu(n+\tfrac12)ℏν(n+21​) are simple and form its whole spectrum, and its resolvent is compact (Theorem 8.8). Evolution: every state returns, up to the sign −1-1−1, after the period 2π/ν2\pi/\nu2π/ν, and a quarter period is a Fourier transform with a change of scale (Proposition 8.9). A coherent state follows the classical orbit without spreading (Theorem 8.10). Measurement: in a coherent state, the level number nnn of an energy reading ℏν(n+12)\hbar\nu(n+\tfrac12)ℏν(n+21​) follows a Poisson distribution with the mean ∣α∣2\abs\alpha^2∣α∣2. Two coherent states cannot be told apart with certainty.
