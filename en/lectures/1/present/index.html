Lecture 1

The particle in a box

A chain with fixed ends suggests the energy rule for a particle in a box. We build the self-adjoint Hamiltonian from the sine basis and prove that its energy postulate determines it uniquely. Energy readings remain defined for states outside its domain. Its evolution is Lecture 2’s subject.

1.2 Wavefunctions in a box

The chain’s state was a vector of amplitudes, one for each site. A state of the particle assigns an amplitude ψ(x)\psi(x)ψ(x) to each position xxx in the box. The function ψ\psiψ is the wavefunction of the state. Lecture 2 postulates that ∣ψ(x)∣2\abs{\psi(x)}^2∣ψ(x)∣2 is the probability density for finding the particle near xxx. The wavefunctions form a vector space with the inner product ⟨φ,ψ⟩=∫0Lφ(x)‾ ψ(x) dx,\begin{equation} \label{eq:1:inner} \inner\varphi\psi=\int_0^L\overline{\varphi(x)}\,\psi(x)\,\dd x , \end{equation}⟨φ,ψ⟩=∫0L​φ(x)​ψ(x)dx,(1.3) which is antilinear in the first slot, as in Quantum Mechanics I. The space is L2(0,L)\Ltwo(0,L)L2(0,L). Its elements are the functions with ∫0L∣ψ∣2dx<∞\int_0^L\abs{\psi}^2\dd x<\infty∫0L​∣ψ∣2dx<∞, and two functions that differ only on a set of zero length count as the same element (Linear Algebra, Def. 13.15). With the norm ∥ψ∥=⟨ψ,ψ⟩1/2\norm\psi=\inner\psi\psi^{1/2}∥ψ∥=⟨ψ,ψ⟩1/2, the space L2(0,L)\Ltwo(0,L)L2(0,L) is a Hilbert space, a complete inner product space.

So an element of L2(0,L)\Ltwo(0,L)L2(0,L) has no value at a single point. Changing ψ\psiψ at x=0x=0x=0 alone changes no integral, and it does not change the element. A condition such as ψ(0)=0\psi(0)=0ψ(0)=0 therefore cannot be imposed on the states. It will return as a condition on the domain of the Hamiltonian.

Postulate 1.2 (States of a particle in a box). The states of a particle in the box are the rays of L2(0,L)\Ltwo(0,L)L2(0,L).

Every state is represented by a sequence of amplitudes, the coefficients of a unit vector along the functions un(x)=2L sin⁡nπxL(n=1,2,3,… ).\begin{equation} \label{eq:1:modes} u_n(x)=\sqrt{\frac2L}\,\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\qquad(n=1,2,3,\dots). \end{equation}un​(x)=L2​​sinLnπx​(n=1,2,3,…).(1.4) The functions unu_nun​ are orthonormal, by the integral of a product of two sines (Exercise Sheet 1).

Every ψ∈L2(0,L)\psi\in\Ltwo(0,L)ψ∈L2(0,L) is the norm-convergent series ψ=∑n=1∞cnun,cn=⟨un,ψ⟩,\begin{equation} \label{eq:1:expansion} \psi=\sum_{n=1}^\infty c_nu_n,\qquad c_n=\inner{u_n}\psi , \end{equation}ψ=n=1∑∞​cn​un​,cn​=⟨un​,ψ⟩,(1.5) and every sequence (cn)(c_n)(cn​) with ∑n∣cn∣2<∞\sum_n\abs{c_n}^2<\infty∑n​∣cn​∣2<∞ is the coefficient sequence of exactly one ψ\psiψ. 1.3 Stationary waves

The chain of §1.1 suggests Schrödinger’s rule: for a free particle of mass mmm, the energy acts on a wavefunction by ψ↦−(ℏ2/2m) ψ′′\psi\mapsto-(\hbar^2/2m)\,\psi''ψ↦−(ℏ2/2m)ψ′′. We take the rule as given here, and §1.5 makes it a postulate. A wavefunction of definite energy EEE, a stationary wave, solves −(ℏ2/2m) u′′=Eu-(\hbar^2/2m)\,u''=Eu−(ℏ2/2m)u′′=Eu. Impenetrable walls are modelled by asking the stationary wave to vanish at both walls, as the chain’s fixed ends suggest. Hall (§3.9) motivates this condition by a very deep well.

The stationary waves are the sine functions unu_nun​ of the sine basis (Figure 1.1, left), and their energies EnE_nEn​ are the limits of the chain’s levels. A classical particle in the box may have any energy E≥0E\ge0E≥0. In a cyanine dye, a chain of carbon atoms carries electrons, the π\piπ electrons, that move along the whole chain. Its bonds are drawn as alternating single and double bonds, but they are of nearly equal length. Kuhn modelled these electrons in 1949 as free particles in a box of the chain’s length LLL. With 2K2K2K such electrons filling the lowest KKK levels, two to a level (Quantum Mechanics I, Lecture 14), the least energy that the electrons can absorb lifts one of them from the level KKK to the level K+1K+1K+1, and it is EK+1−EK=(2K+1)E1E_{K+1}-E_K=(2K+1)E_1EK+1​−EK​=(2K+1)E1​. For K=5K=5K=5 and L=1.4 nmL=1.4\,\mathrm{nm}L=1.4nm the model predicts the absorption of light of wavelength about 590 nm590\,\mathrm{nm}590nm, which is yellow. Such dyes do absorb visible light, the longer chains at longer wavelengths. The model works less well for chains whose bond lengths alternate, such as those of the polyenes (Autschbach, 2007).

1.4 Why the energy needs a domain

The energies grow without bound, En=n2E1E_n=n^2E_1En​=n2E1​, and this is where the new difficulty comes from. In the coefficients of (1.5), an energy that multiplies each stationary wave unu_nun​ by EnE_nEn​ multiplies each amplitude cnc_ncn​ by EnE_nEn​. For some states the result is not square-summable, so it is the coefficient sequence of no vector of L2(0,L)\Ltwo(0,L)L2(0,L). §1.7 gives such a state, with cn∝1/n2c_n\propto1/n^2cn​∝1/n2. The energy multiplies the unit vector unu_nun​ by EnE_nEn​, so it is not bounded. By the Hellinger–Toeplitz theorem of §0.1, it cannot be a symmetric operator defined on every vector.

Recall from Lecture 0 the operators with a domain (Definition 0.1), the adjoint A†\adj AA† of a densely defined operator AAA (Definition 0.2), and the symmetric and the self-adjoint operators (Definition 0.3). A symmetric operator AAA has D(A)⊆D(A†)\dom A\subseteq\dom{\adj A}D(A)⊆D(A†), with A†φ=Aφ\adj A\varphi=A\varphiA†φ=Aφ there, and it is self-adjoint when the two domains are equal.

Postulate 1.5 (Observables with an eigenbasis). The observables of a particle in the box are self-adjoint operators on L2(0,L)\Ltwo(0,L)L2(0,L). Suppose an observable AAA has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors e1,e2,…e_1,e_2,\dotse1​,e2​,…, with Aek=akekAe_k=a_ke_kAek​=ak​ek​. Then its readings are the numbers aka_kak​, and the measurement postulate of Quantum Mechanics I (Born’s and Lüders’ rules, Postulate 4.1) holds, with the projection EaE_aEa​ (not an energy) Eaψ=∑k: ak=a⟨ek,ψ⟩ ek,Prob⁡(a)=∥Eaψ∥2=∑k: ak=a∣⟨ek,ψ⟩∣2.\begin{equation} \label{eq:1:Ea} E_a\psi=\sum_{k:\,a_k=a}\inner{e_k}\psi\,e_k ,\qquad \Prob(a)=\norm{E_a\psi}^2=\sum_{k:\,a_k=a}\abs{\inner{e_k}\psi}^2 . \end{equation}Ea​ψ=k:ak​=a∑​⟨ek​,ψ⟩ek​,Prob(a)=∥Ea​ψ∥2=k:ak​=a∑​∣⟨ek​,ψ⟩∣2.(1.9)

Symmetry alone does not make an observable. Physics texts often call an operator Hermitian when it is symmetric. In infinite dimension the two notions differ. Integration by parts, as in the step on the rule, proves that Schrödinger’s rule on the twice continuously differentiable functions that vanish at the walls is symmetric, and it is not self-adjoint there (Exercise Sheet 1). Summary

  • The open chain: with κ=ℏ2/2ma2\kappa=\hbar^2/2ma^2κ=ℏ2/2ma2, its levels 4κsin⁡2(nπ/2N)4\kappa\sin^2(n\pi/2N)4κsin2(nπ/2N) approach the box’s energies En=n2E1E_n=n^2E_1En​=n2E1​ at each fixed nnn as the sites come closer (Proposition 1.1).
  • The box Hamiltonian is self-adjoint on its domain, and its eigenvalues are the EnE_nEn​. It is the only self-adjoint operator that obeys the energy postulate (Theorem 1.8).
  • A state outside the domain: the domain holds the states whose energy readings have a finite mean square.
