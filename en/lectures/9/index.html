9.5 What the theorem decides

The theorem answers the question of this lecture. A particle on the line without further degrees of freedom has the state space L2(R)\Ltwo(\R)L2(R), with QQQ and PPP on their domains of Theorem 4.6. This holds as soon as its translations in position and in momentum are strongly continuous, obey Weyl’s relation and act irreducibly. No other realisation exists, up to a unitary map.

Besides the continuity of the two groups, it assumes three things. Weyl’s relation, not the commutator on a dense domain, as the box shows. Irreducibility, which excludes a spin. One value of ℏ\hbarℏ. It decides nothing about the Hamiltonian. The free particle and the oscillator share the pair and differ in their energies.

The canonical pair

States: for a particle on the line with no further degree of freedom, every irreducible Weyl pair is the line’s, up to a unitary map (Theorem 9.5). So its state space is L2(R)\Ltwo(\R)L2(R). Observables: the generators of the two groups are P/ℏP/\hbarP/ℏ and −Q/ℏ-Q/\hbar−Q/ℏ, with the domains of PPP and QQQ, and U(a)V(b)=e−iab/ℏV(b)U(a)U(a)V(b)=e^{-\ii ab/\hbar}V(b)U(a)U(a)V(b)=e−iab/ℏV(b)U(a) (Proposition 9.2).
