Lecture 4

The free particle: observables without eigenvalues

The Fourier transform builds the position, momentum and free energy on the line. These observables are self-adjoint and have no eigenvalues. For every unit vector in S(R)\mathcal S(\R)S(R), we prove uncertainty with equality exactly for the Gaussian packets. In a box the same inequality fails.

By the symmetry of QQQ and PPP and by Proposition 4.7, ⟨Qψ,Pψ⟩−⟨Pψ,Qψ⟩=⟨ψ,QPψ−PQψ⟩=iℏ.\inner{Q\psi}{P\psi}-\inner{P\psi}{Q\psi}=\inner\psi{QP\psi-PQ\psi}=\ii\hbar .⟨Qψ,Pψ⟩−⟨Pψ,Qψ⟩=⟨ψ,QPψ−PQψ⟩=iℏ. Lemma 4.8 gives ΔQ ΔP≥ℏ/2\Delta Q\,\Delta P\ge\hbar/2ΔQΔP≥ℏ/2.

Next, equality. By the same proof, ΔQ ΔP≥∣z∣≥Im z=ℏ2.\Delta Q\,\Delta P\ge\abs z\ge\mathrm{Im}\,z=\frac\hbar2 .ΔQΔP≥∣z∣≥Imz=2ℏ​. Equality needs both steps to be equalities. The second gives z=iℏ/2z=\ii\hbar/2z=iℏ/2. Conversely, let ψ\psiψ be (4.3). Then ∣ψ(x)∣2=(2πs2)−1/2e−(x−q)2/2s2\abs{\psi(x)}^2=(2\pi s^2)^{-1/2}e^{-(x-q)^2/2s^2}∣ψ(x)∣2=(2πs2)−1/2e−(x−q)2/2s2, a normal density with the mean qqq and the spread sss, so ⟨Q⟩=q\langle Q\rangle=q⟨Q⟩=q and ΔQ=s\Delta Q=sΔQ=s. Differentiating, −iℏψ′=p0ψ+(iℏ/2s2)(x−q)ψ-\ii\hbar\psi'=p_0\psi+(\ii\hbar/2s^2)(x-q)\psi−iℏψ′=p0​ψ+(iℏ/2s2)(x−q)ψ, so (P−p0)ψ=(iℏ/2s2)(Q−q)ψ(P-p_0)\psi=(\ii\hbar/2s^2)(Q-q)\psi(P−p0​)ψ=(iℏ/2s2)(Q−q)ψ. Galindo and Pascual, (3.39)–(3.42), find the same packets. Both spreads are finite exactly on D(Q)∩D(P)\dom Q\cap\dom PD(Q)∩D(P), the natural domain of the inequality, and there it holds too, by a cited theorem.

Theorem 4.10 (Uncertainty on the natural domainscited). For every unit vector ψ∈D(Q)∩D(P)\psi\in\dom Q\cap\dom Pψ∈D(Q)∩D(P), ΔQ ΔP≥ℏ/2\Delta Q\,\Delta P\ge\hbar/2ΔQΔP≥ℏ/2.

Hall, Thm 12.7, proves it without assuming ψ∈D(QP)∩D(PQ)\psi\in\dom{QP}\cap\dom{PQ}ψ∈D(QP)∩D(PQ). His proof writes PPP through the translations of Lecture 9, and it gives ⟨Qψ,Pψ⟩−⟨Pψ,Qψ⟩=iℏ\inner{Q\psi}{P\psi}-\inner{P\psi}{Q\psi}=\ii\hbar⟨Qψ,Pψ⟩−⟨Pψ,Qψ⟩=iℏ for unit vectors in D(Q)∩D(P)\dom Q\cap\dom PD(Q)∩D(P) (his (12.19)–(12.20)). With this identity, the proof of equality above applies up to the differential equation. By Hall, Prop. 9.32, that equation now holds for a derivative in a weaker sense. By Hall, Prop. A.30, a function whose derivative in that sense is 000 is constant. So on D(Q)∩D(P)\dom Q\cap\dom PD(Q)∩D(P), too, equality holds only for the packets (4.3).

The line is essential to this theorem. Robertson’s inequality holds in the box too. There the position, which we also write QQQ, multiplies by xxx on L2(0,L)\Ltwo(0,L)L2(0,L). Since ∥Qψ∥≤L∥ψ∥\norm{Q\psi}\le L\norm\psi∥Qψ∥≤L∥ψ∥, this QQQ is bounded and defined on every vector. Since xxx is real, this QQQ is self-adjoint (Linear Algebra, Ex. 14.6). For the momenta PθP_\thetaPθ​ of Definition 3.9 a boundary term enters.

Proposition 4.11 (The boundary term). For every θ\thetaθ and every ψ∈D(Pθ)\psi\in\dom{P_\theta}ψ∈D(Pθ​), ⟨Qψ,Pθψ⟩−⟨Pθψ,Qψ⟩=iℏ(∥ψ∥2−L∣ψ(L)∣2).\inner{Q\psi}{P_\theta\psi}-\inner{P_\theta\psi}{Q\psi} =\ii\hbar\bigl(\norm\psi^2-L\abs{\psi(L)}^2\bigr).⟨Qψ,Pθ​ψ⟩−⟨Pθ​ψ,Qψ⟩=iℏ(∥ψ∥2−L∣ψ(L)∣2).

Proof. By the product rule for A\mathcal AA (§3.2), QψQ\psiQψ lies in A\mathcal AA, with (Qψ)′=ψ+xψ′(Q\psi)'=\psi+x\psi'(Qψ)′=ψ+xψ′, but in general not in D(Pθ)\dom{P_\theta}D(Pθ​). The first inner product is ⟨Qψ,Pθψ⟩\inner{Q\psi}{P_\theta\psi}⟨Qψ,Pθ​ψ⟩. Since xxx is real, the second is iℏ∥ψ∥2+⟨Pθψ,Qψ⟩\ii\hbar\norm\psi^2+\inner{P_\theta\psi}{Q\psi}iℏ∥ψ∥2+⟨Pθ​ψ,Qψ⟩. Moving iℏ∥ψ∥2\ii\hbar\norm\psi^2iℏ∥ψ∥2 to the right side gives the claim. 

So for a unit vector ψ∈D(Pθ)\psi\in\dom{P_\theta}ψ∈D(Pθ​), Robertson’s inequality gives ΔQ ΔPθ≥(ℏ/2)∣1−L∣ψ(L)∣2∣\Delta Q\,\Delta P_\theta\ge(\hbar/2)\abs{1-L\abs{\psi(L)}^2}ΔQΔPθ​≥(ℏ/2)​1−L∣ψ(L)∣2​. For the eigenvector eθ,ke_{\theta,k}eθ,k​ of Theorem 3.10, L∣eθ,k(L)∣2=1L\abs{e_{\theta,k}(L)}^2=1L∣eθ,k​(L)∣2=1, and indeed ΔPθ=0\Delta P_\theta=0ΔPθ​=0 while ΔQ=L/12\Delta Q=L/\sqrt{12}ΔQ=L/12​ is finite. So the product is 000. In a box, ΔQ ΔP≥ℏ/2\Delta Q\,\Delta P\ge\hbar/2ΔQΔP≥ℏ/2 fails. On D(PD)\dom{P_D}D(PD​), where ψ(L)=0\psi(L)=0ψ(L)=0, the inequality holds. Hall, §12.2, gives the same example. The line has no walls and no boundary term.

The spreads belong to separate runs, as in Quantum Mechanics I, Lecture 5. On some copies of one preparation the position is read, and on others the momentum. Theorem 4.9 says that no preparation makes both spreads small. It says nothing about a position reading disturbing a later momentum reading.

4.5 A Gaussian’s momenta

The momentum readings of a Gaussian packet are found from the transform of (4.3).

So the first diffraction maxima lie about 25 μ25\,\mu25μrad from the beam, and the experiment resolved them.

The free particle

Observables: the position QQQ, the momentum P=F−1MpFP=F^{-1}M_pFP=F−1Mp​F, with FFF the Fourier transform (Theorem 4.2), and the free energy H0=P2/2mH_0=P^2/2mH0​=P2/2m are self-adjoint, and none has an eigenvalue. The densities ∣ψ∣2\abs\psi^2∣ψ∣2 and ∣ψ~∣2\abs{\fourier\psi}^2​ψ~​​2 give the readings of QQQ and PPP on intervals (Theorem 4.6 and Postulate 4.5). A plane wave is not a state. The canonical relation: QPψ−PQψ=iℏψQP\psi-PQ\psi=\ii\hbar\psiQPψ−PQψ=iℏψ for every ψ∈D(QP)∩D(PQ)\psi\in\dom{QP}\cap\dom{PQ}ψ∈D(QP)∩D(PQ) (Proposition 4.7). Uncertainty: for unit ψ∈S(R)\psi\in\mathcal S(\R)ψ∈S(R), ΔQ ΔP≥ℏ/2\Delta Q\,\Delta P\ge\hbar/2ΔQΔP≥ℏ/2, with equality exactly for the Gaussian packets (4.3), whose momentum density is normal with the spread ℏ/2s\hbar/2sℏ/2s (Theorem 4.9 and Example 4.12). In a box the inequality fails (Proposition 4.11).

Summary

  • The transform of a derivative: on S(R)\mathcal S(\R)S(R), the transform of −iℏψ′-\ii\hbar\psi'−iℏψ′ is pψ~p\fourier\psipψ~​ (Proposition 4.3), and Plancherel makes the transform unitary (Theorem 4.2).
  • Position, momentum and free energy are self-adjoint, none has an eigenvalue, and H0=P2/2mH_0=P^2/2mH0​=P2/2m (Theorem 4.6).
  • The canonical commutation relation holds on S(R)\mathcal S(\R)S(R) and on D(QP)∩D(PQ)\dom{QP}\cap\dom{PQ}D(QP)∩D(PQ) (Proposition 4.7).
  • The uncertainty relation: ΔQ ΔP≥ℏ/2\Delta Q\,\Delta P\ge\hbar/2ΔQΔP≥ℏ/2 for unit ψ∈S(R)\psi\in\mathcal S(\R)ψ∈S(R), with equality exactly for the Gaussian packets (Theorem 4.9), and on D(Q)∩D(P)\dom Q\cap\dom PD(Q)∩D(P) by a cited theorem (Theorem 4.10). In a box, the boundary term breaks it (Proposition 4.11).
  • The momentum distribution of a Gaussian packet is normal, with the spread ℏ/2s\hbar/2sℏ/2s (Example 4.12).

Lecture 5 puts the readings of QQQ and PPP on intervals into one rule for every self-adjoint operator, the spectral measure.
