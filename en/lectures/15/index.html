Hydrogen: the spectrum

What this lecture does

Lecture 14 proved that the Hamiltonian HHH of hydrogen is self-adjoint, that its spectrum lies in [−Ry,∞)[-\mathrm{Ry},\infty)[−Ry,∞) and contains [0,∞)[0,\infty)[0,∞), and that every negative spectral point is an eigenvalue. We find these eigenvalues, prove their multiplicities and compare their transitions with the Balmer lines.

15.4 What a continuous quantum system is

This course asked the four questions of Quantum Mechanics I for a particle, and the continuum changed each answer.

States. A state is the ray of a unit vector in a space of infinite dimension, L2\LtwoL2 of an interval, of the line or of space. Its vectors are classes of functions, and a position has no eigenvector in it.

Observables. An observable is a self-adjoint operator, with its domain. A symmetric operator is not enough. The momentum PDP_DPD​ in a box is symmetric and not self-adjoint, and its self-adjoint versions PθP_\thetaPθ​ give different readings (Lecture 3). Nor is a formula enough. Hydrogen’s rule −(ℏ2/2m)Δ−C/∣x∣-(\hbar^2/2m)\Delta-C/\abs x−(ℏ2/2m)Δ−C/∣x∣ is self-adjoint on D(H0)\dom{H_0}D(H0​) with the least energy −Ry-\mathrm{Ry}−Ry, and its other self-adjoint extensions from the smooth functions that vanish near the origin each have a level below −Ry-\mathrm{Ry}−Ry (§14.2). The spectrum of a self-adjoint operator is the set of numbers with approximate eigenvectors (Theorem 6.5). It is discrete in the box and the oscillator, continuous for the free particle, and has both parts in the finite well and in hydrogen.

Measurement. A reading asks whether an observable lies in an interval, and the spectral measure gives its probability (Postulate 5.5).

Evolution. Evolution is a strongly continuous unitary group, and its generator is self-adjoint (Stone, Theorem 7.8). For a potential on the line, the states of Hpp\Hil_{\mathrm{pp}}Hpp​ stay near the well, and every state Ωoutχ\Omega_{\mathrm{out}}\chiΩout​χ leaves every bounded interval (Theorem 12.6).

Foundations takes up what this course left out: states that are density operators on an infinite-dimensional space, readings more general than spectral measures, and the question of what an observation is.

Hydrogen

States: the levels −Ry/n2-\mathrm{Ry}/n^2−Ry/n2 have the eigenvectors p(x)v(∣x∣)e−∣x∣/na0p(x)v(\abs x)e^{-\abs x/na_0}p(x)v(∣x∣)e−∣x∣/na0​, p∈Vlp\in\mathcal V_lp∈Vl​, l≤n−1l\le n-1l≤n−1 (Theorem 15.1), and these span each eigenspace (Theorem 15.3). Measurement: a reading of the energy in an interval that misses {−Ry/n2}∪[0,∞)\{-\mathrm{Ry}/n^2\}\cup[0,\infty){−Ry/n2}∪[0,∞) answers yes with probability 000 (Theorem 15.3, and Theorem 6.6, cited).
